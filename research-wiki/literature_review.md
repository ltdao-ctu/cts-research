## Landscape Synthesis

### 1. Evolution of Ultrasound Deep Learning: From Static CSA Segmentation to End-to-End & Comparative Diagnosis
Early deep learning work in CTS focused on automating median nerve (MN) boundary delineation at the carpal tunnel inlet to replace operator-dependent manual tracing of cross-sectional area (CSA). Architectures based on Mask R-CNN (Smerilli et al., 2022; Di Cosmo et al., 2021, 2022) and U-Net (Festen et al., 2021) reached pooled Dice scores of `0.88–0.91` (Wang et al., 2023). Subsequent studies extended static segmentation to dynamic cine-loop tracking using ConvLSTM and Siamese similarity learning (Horng et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2020) and unified segmentation, biometric measurement, and classification into a single real-time pipeline (Peng et al., 2024).

### 2. Consensus vs. Disagreements: Morphological Cutoffs vs. Intrinsic Echotexture
A central tension in the literature is whether CTS diagnosis should rely on explicit geometric measurements (CSA thresholds of `9–12 mm²`) or direct appearance/echotexture representations. Faeghi et al. (2021) and the meta-analysis by Wu et al. (2023) showed that intraneural ultrasound radiomics (wavelet/texture descriptors) achieve `~88%` sensitivity and specificity, surpassing visual assessment. Shinohara et al. (2022) demonstrated that CSA-free CNNs achieve up to `96%` accuracy by attending to the epineurium and surrounding tissues, while Sim et al. (2025) introduced cosine-similarity comparative guidance between median-innervated thenar and ulnar-innervated hypothenar muscles to eliminate inter-subject anatomical bias altogether.

### 3. Electrodiagnostic Signal Learning and Multimodal Prognosis
On the electrodiagnostic (EDX/NCS) side, recent work has moved beyond tabular latency/amplitude thresholds (Tsamis et al., 2021; Park et al., 2021; Öten & Aygün, 2024) toward time-frequency deep learning directly on raw motor and sensory nerve conduction waveforms using STFT spectrograms and multilevel wavelet decomposition (Bakalis et al., 2024, reaching `94%–97.1%` grading accuracy). Most recently, the field has begun shifting from binary screening to 4-level NCS-anchored ultrasound grading (Malo-Urriés et al., 2026) and multicenter multimodal outcome prediction following surgical or ultrasound-guided needle release (Shi et al., 2026; Manoochehri et al., 2025).
