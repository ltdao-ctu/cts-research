## 2. Deep Full-Text Synthesis (`effort: max`) — Critical Insights Only Visible from Full-Text Audit

Reading all 221 pages across the 15 local PDFs uncovered **five critical technical findings** that are invisible in abstracts/titles and directly reshape our research strategy:

### Finding 1: The "93%–96% NCS Accuracy" Illusion — Circular Label Leakage vs. True Waveform Geometry (`L01`, `L02` vs. `L03`)
- **What Abstracts Claim**: Both Darol et al. (`L01`, *Acta Medica Nicomedia* 2023) and Yener et al. (`L02`, *J. Anat. Soc. India* 2024) report `93.55%` (4-class) and `96.0%` (binary, `0.99` AUC) SVM accuracy on 315 patients using 17 demographic and ENMG attributes.
- **What Full-Text Inspection Reveals**:
  1. `L01` and `L02` analyze the **exact same 315-patient cohort** from Sakarya Training and Research Hospital (`n=157` Grade 0, `n=92` Grade 1, `n=55` Grade 2, `n=11` Grade 3).
  2. The ground-truth labels in `L01`/`L02` are Padua electrodiagnostic grades, which are **deterministically defined by threshold rules on Median SCV (`>49 m/s`), Median MDL (`<4.20 ms`), and SNAP/CMAP amplitudes** — the exact same 10 numbers passed as input features to the SVM! Thus, the SVM is merely re-approximating a deterministic clinical lookup table on a tiny hold-out split (`n=31` in `L01` with only **1 Severe patient** in the test set, and `n=64` in `L02` with no cross-validation).
  3. **By contrast, Tsamis et al. (`L03`, *Bioengineering* 2021)** evaluated models against both NCS grading *and* an **independent clinical gold standard** across 65 hands. When tested against independent clinical diagnosis, standard NCS features dropped from `95.13%` to `86.91%` (2-class) and from `94.04%` to `68.67%` (3-class). However, Tsamis et al.'s **23 novel physiological waveform geometry features** (specifically the descending repolarization tangent slope $\tan\theta_{\text{right}}$, expanded sensory area $|A_e^s|$, and subarea ratios reflecting slow potassium-channel axonal repolarization) **outperformed standard NCS features on independent clinical diagnosis** (`87.48%` vs `86.91%` in 2-class; `72.73%` vs `68.67%` in 3-class) and boosted combined 4-class accuracy from `82.43%` to **`91.17%`**.

### Finding 2: Why Longitudinal Ultrasound Sweep Ratios Crush Static Single-Frame Biometrics (`L06` + `L05`)
- **What Full-Text Inspection of Peng et al. (`L06`, `arXiv:2402.05554`) Reveals**:
  - Across **32,301 annotated ultrasound frames** from 130 continuous 5-cm wrist sweeps (~248 frames/video), Peng et al. computed both frame-level measurements (CSA, Perimeter, Anteroposterior Diameter AD, Flattening Ratio FR) and video-level proximal-to-distal ratios (**Swelling Ratio `SR`**, Perimeter Ratio `PR`, `ADR`, `Max CSA`, `Max FR`).
  - In their Random Forest ablation (Tables 4 & 5 of `L06`), **Max Flattening Ratio (`Max FR`) alone achieved an AUC of only `0.57` (`53.85%` accuracy) and contributed just `0.6%` (`0.006`) of total feature importance!** Even `Max CSA` contributed only `14.3%` (`0.89` AUC alone).
  - Conversely, the **longitudinal Swelling Ratio (`SR` = ratio of maximum to minimum CSA along the carpal tunnel sweep)** accounted for **`42.9%` (`0.429`) of total feature importance** and achieved **`0.96` AUC (`92.31%` accuracy) as a single feature** (rising to `93.85%` accuracy and `0.98` AUC when combined with `PR` and `ADR`).
  - Furthermore, `L06` exposed why standard CNNs (`U-Net`) fail on sweep ratios: at deep distal frames (e.g., frame 219/307 where the median nerve dives deeper and echoes weaken), `U-Net` produces fragmented false positives that blow up the denominator of ratio metrics, resulting in a **`262.95%` Perimeter Ratio MAE for `U-Net` vs. `19.57%` for `SegFormer-B2`**!
  - Complementing `L06`, **Park et al. (`L05`, *Scientific Reports* 2021, $N=1,037$ hands)** proved that retinacular **Palmar Bowing (`PB`)** (`2.1` mm in Mild vs `2.6` mm in Moderate vs `3.1` mm in Severe) ranked **higher in both Random Forest and XGBoost feature importance than static `CSA`** (`13.2` vs `15.4` vs `18.9` mm²) for 3-class severity grading.

### Finding 3: The "Moderate CTS" Severity Bottleneck and How Label Distribution Learning Solves It (`L05` + `L15`)
- **The Clinical Bottleneck in `L05`**: In the largest severity-grading study to date (`1,037` CTS hands), Park et al. (`L05`) showed that 3-class XGBoost plateaus at **`76.6%` accuracy** strictly because of **Moderate CTS** (`Balanced Accuracy = 71.8%` vs `83.6%` for Mild and `83.5%` for Severe; in OvR stacked ensemble + SMOTE, Moderate-vs-Rest sensitivity is only **`60.0%`** with PPV **`54.9%`**, whereas Severe-vs-Rest reaches **`90.9%` accuracy and `0.95` ROC**).
- **The Mathematical Solution in `L15` (`arXiv:2006.00202`)**: Chen et al. (`L15`) demonstrated on `12,600` wrist radiographs that when adjacent clinical stages lie on a continuous biological progression, hard one-hot classification or raw $\ell_1$ regression suffers from boundary ambiguity. By converting each target grade $t$ into a **Gaussian Label Distribution $G_i(\delta)$** and optimizing **Joint KL-Divergence Label Distribution Learning + Expectation Regression ($\ell = \ell_{\text{MAE}} + \lambda D_{\text{KL}}(p_i \parallel G_i)$)** focused on the attention-localized carpal region (`R1`), error dropped from `7.3` to **`4.3` months MAE**. Integrating `L15`'s Gaussian Label Distribution Learning + Expectation Regression directly into **Idea 002 (Neuro-Sonographic Cross-Modal Distillation & Ordinal Severity Calibration)** provides a principled remedy for `L05`'s Moderate-grade bottleneck.

### Finding 4: Multi-Class Anatomical Segmentation Pitfall & Scanner Domain Shift (`L08` + `L09` + `L10`)
- **The Multi-Class Trap (`L08`, `arXiv:2602.00763`)**: When Yves et al. (`L08`) extended `U-Net` from binary nerve segmentation to 4-class anatomical segmentation (artery, vein, nerve, muscle) across 955 ultrasound images from two scanner vendors (**Siemens ACUSON NX3 Elite `U1`** vs **Philips EPIQ5 `U2`**), **nerve Dice plunged by `-61%` on `U1` (`0.274 -> 0.107`), `-11%` on `U2` (`0.407 -> 0.363`), and `-9%` on `U1+U2` (`0.388 -> 0.355`)**. Large, high-contrast muscle regions dominated pixel-wise cross-entropy gradients and competed with small nerve boundaries (`Pearson r = 0.587` between nerve pixel area and Dice).
- **Design Consequence for Our Architecture (`Idea 001` & `Idea 003`)**:
  1. Never train a naive shared pixel-wise cross-entropy head across median nerve, flexor tendons, and carpal tunnel muscle/ligament simultaneously. Instead, use **decoupled binary/anatomical query heads** (or hierarchical anatomical prompting where carpal bone/retinaculum landmarks condition a dedicated nerve segmentation head) weighted by boundary/Dice-Focal losses.
  2. To overcome scanner-vendor domain shift (`Siemens` vs `Philips` gap of `0.274` vs `0.407` in `L08`), incorporate **Tripathi et al.'s (`L10`, `arXiv:2109.06346`) physics-driven monogenic phase-symmetry (`LP × FS × (1 - IBS)`) and directional Radon representation** alongside **Bello et al.'s (`L09`, `arXiv:2603.05541`) Keyed Latent Transform (`KLT`) + Unified Mapping Network (`UMN`)** (`76.90%` nerve DSC across 5,635 images at `19.07 ms` latency with zero raw image sharing).

### Finding 5: Robust Dynamic Trajectory Regression & Sample-Size Sufficiency (`L11` + `L12` + `L13` + `L14`)
- **Outlier-Resistant Kinematic Regression (`L12` + `L13`)**: Zarenia et al. (`L12`) and Sharafi et al. (`L13`, $N=49$ subjects) showed that dynamic wrist imaging suffers from intermittent slice/frame segmentation outliers (echoing `L06`'s finding where a single bad distal ultrasound frame ruined `U-Net`'s Perimeter Ratio). `L13` solved this by applying **Modified $z$-score outlier frame rejection ($\text{Modified } z = 0.675 |c_i - \tilde{c}|/\text{MAD} > 3.5$)** followed by **1st- and 2nd-order polynomial trajectory regression**, discovering that **quadratic curvature coefficients** exhibit the sharpest stability breakdown between healthy (`25` stable metrics with `ICC > 0.7`) and pathological wrists (`12` stable metrics). Applying Modified-$z$ outlier rejection + polynomial profile fitting along the 248-frame longitudinal ultrasound sweep in `L06` will eliminate single-frame `Max CSA` / `Swelling Ratio` spikes!
- **Sample Complexity (`L14`)**: Silva et al. (`L14`) proved across 16 dataset scales that **`N >= 96` annotated wrist images** (48 positive, 48 negative) are sufficient to reach **`mAP@0.5 > 0.95`** (and `0.990` at $n=240$), even for tiny carpal structures occupying just **`0.1%` of total image area**.
